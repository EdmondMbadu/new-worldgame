This week’s sample topic

Clean energy for healthier communities

Energy can feel like a technical subject, but its effects are deeply human. Reliable, cleaner power can help clinics protect medicine, schools keep learning spaces comfortable, and neighborhoods stay connected during disruptions.

Why it matters

Reliable energy supports health, education, communication, safety, and local economic activity.

What to watch

Look for projects that connect practical energy upgrades with measurable community benefits.

One step this week

Identify one local building where better energy reliability could improve daily life, then learn who manages it.

Funding pathways

Organizations that can help fund the work

Eligibility and application windows change. Always confirm the current cycle on the funder’s website.

DOE Clean Cities and Communities

Current listings

A regularly updated directory of federal and partner funding for cleaner transportation, alternative fuels, and community deployment projects.

Best for: local governments, coalitions, fleets, nonprofits, and public-private partners.

United States

DOE Community Microgrid Assistance Partnership

Rolling support

C-MAP offers on-demand technical support and periodic direct funding for microgrids serving remote, rural, island, and Tribal communities.

Status: the 2026 funding round is closed, but tailored support remains available and future rounds are posted here.

United States

EPA Environmental and Climate Justice Program

Local cycles

EPA’s Thriving Communities network works through regional grantmakers to provide community subgrants and technical assistance with fewer application barriers.

Best for: community-based organizations and underserved or overburdened communities. Check the relevant regional grantmaker.

Find EPA community funding ↗

International

Green Climate Fund

Global pathway

The GCF finances climate projects in developing countries and offers project-preparation support to help institutions develop investment-ready proposals.

Access is through an accredited entity or the project-specific assessment pathway—not through individual applications.
