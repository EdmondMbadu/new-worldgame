§ 01 · Host audience

For schools, departments, student groups, and community partners.

This page is for people deciding whether their school or organization can host a hub. It is not the student registration page.

A hub can be a university classroom, high school innovation space, library, nonprofit office, or community venue. The important pieces are a committed local organizer, enough student teams, reliable connectivity, and a full-day space.

If you need the event format, schedule, divisions, judging rubric, and prize structure, review the main Solution-a-thon program page.

§ 02 · Hub model

Local space. Shared national experience.

GSL provides the event structure and platform. The hub provides the local room, recruiting, and day-of coordination.

GSL provides

The sprint infrastructure.

We provide the shared structure that lets every hub work on the same clock.

  • Event structure and schedule
  • Track materials and judging rubric
  • Global Solutions Lab platform access
  • National livestream connection
  • Post-event coordination

Hub provides

The local student experience.

You create the place where teams can focus, collaborate, and stay supported through the day.

  • Local room and day-of coordination
  • Recruiting student teams
  • Local check-in and support
  • Optional food or reception
  • Campus or community promotion

§ 03 · Host conversation

What we should confirm before listing a hub.

01

Expected number of teams and the student audience you can reach.

02

Venue size, room access, A/V, WiFi, and day-of support constraints.

03

Local check-in, food, promotion, and student communication responsibilities.

04

Judge, mentor, faculty, or community partner availability.

05

How hosting connects to your school's learning, service, or innovation goals.
