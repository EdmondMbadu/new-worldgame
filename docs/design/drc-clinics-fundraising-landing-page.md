# Power the Next 15 Clinics

Fundraising landing-page strategy and implementation brief for the Global Solutions Lab DRC team.

Concept image: [drc-clinics-fundraising-landing-page-concept.png](./drc-clinics-fundraising-landing-page-concept.png)

## The central idea

This campaign should not lead with a vast problem. It should lead with a completed promise:

> One rural clinic was powered. The lights came on. Care changed that night. Now help the same local team reach 15 more clinics.

The page should make a donor feel three things in this order:

1. **This matters now.** Reliable electricity makes safer births, vaccine storage, diagnostics, surgery, emergency response, and care after dark possible.
2. **This team can do it.** Ndingi Clinic is the visible, documented proof of concept.
3. **My contribution has a clear destination.** Each clinic has a verified profile, a transparent budget, milestones, and follow-up reporting.

The design must preserve dignity. It should feel like an ambitious infrastructure and health campaign led by capable local people, not like a generic charity appeal.

## Recommended campaign identity

- **Campaign name:** Power the Next 15 Clinics
- **Campaign line:** Reliable power. Safer care. Stronger communities.
- **Primary CTA:** Fund a clinic
- **Secondary CTA:** See the Ndingi story
- **Route:** `/campaigns/power-drc-clinics`
- **Languages at launch:** English and French
- **Campaign goal:** $230,000 recommended public target
- **Public cost language:** About $15,000 per clinic
- **Internal planning figure:** $15,376.24 per clinic from the current detailed expansion budget; 15 clinics total $230,643.60

Use a campaign-specific donation destination or campaign code. The existing generic Global Solutions Lab donation link can be a fallback, but donors should not have to wonder whether their gift is allocated to the DRC clinic campaign.

## Page architecture

### 1. Header

- Global Solutions Lab / EarthGame identity
- The Pilot
- The 15 Clinics
- Our Plan
- Transparency
- EN / FR
- Persistent Donate button

The mobile version should have a sticky bottom CTA showing campaign progress and “Donate”.

### 2. Hero: proof before promise

Use the real Ndingi Clinic photography as an asymmetric collage:

- Clinic before work
- Renovated clinic
- Solar panels being installed
- Local installation team
- Interior illuminated at night

Show one clear number, one primary CTA, and one emotional promise. Do not place a long paragraph above the fold.

### 3. The night Ndingi changed

**Section title:** The night Ndingi changed.

Use a before / after / at-night sequence. The strongest current copy is:

> The impact was immediate: nighttime deliveries, surgeries, and emergency care could take place under stable light.

Tell the story as a short timeline:

1. The team assessed the clinic remotely from building photographs.
2. Equipment was purchased from a local Kinshasa supplier.
3. It traveled through Boma and then 11 difficult hours to Ndingi by 4x4.
4. Local installation covered the maternity ward, laboratory, inpatient rooms, consultation office, operating room, reception, and second inpatient building.
5. Technical testing confirmed generation, storage, nighttime stability, and distribution.

**Pilot specification:** four 500W solar panels; 2,000W installed capacity; battery storage; charge controller; inverter; hybrid operation day and night.

### 4. Why electricity changes care

Use three restrained impact statements rather than an overwhelming statistics wall:

- **Care after dark:** lighting supports childbirth, surgery, consultations, and emergency response.
- **Equipment that works:** reliable electricity enables diagnostics, sterilization, refrigeration, communication, and other critical equipment.
- **A fairer health system:** rural clinics can offer more of the dependable services available in urban facilities.

Recommended source-backed global context:

> Close to 1 billion people in low- and lower-middle-income countries are served by health-care facilities with no electricity or unreliable electricity. In sub-Saharan Africa, only 40% of facilities have reliable electricity.

Source: [World Health Organization — Electricity in health-care facilities](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/electricity-in-health-care-facilities)

The campaign should distinguish this global/regional context from claims specifically measured at the campaign clinics.

### 5. Fifteen clinics, one clear path

Show a campaign progress line:

`1 proven pilot → 15 clinics next`

Each target clinic gets a real, donor-readable profile. Never publish invented placeholder facts. Until field verification is complete, use “Profile coming after field verification.”

Required profile fields:

- Clinic name and facility type
- Province, territory, and village; public map precision appropriate for safety
- Verification date and verifier
- Current source and reliability of electricity
- Services provided, including maternity, laboratory, inpatient, surgical, or vaccination services
- Staff count
- Approximate patient visits or catchment population, with source and date
- Current building condition
- Equipment currently blocked by lack of power
- Proposed system size and energy audit status
- Clinic-specific budget
- Readiness status: identified, assessment scheduled, verified, funded, installation scheduled, powered, monitoring
- Before photos and permission status
- Local implementation partner
- 30-day, 90-day, and one-year updates after commissioning

| Unforeseen costs / contingency | $1,397.84 |
| **Total per clinic** | **$15,376.24** |

Recommended donor amounts should be tied to real budget lines, with a note that gifts are pooled unless the donation is legally restricted:

- $50 — lighting and sockets
- $350 — Starlink kit
- $400 — clinic laptop
- $480 — refrigerator
- $550 — one 400Ah battery
- $1,200 — hybrid inverter
- $3,663 — nine 500W panels
- $15,376 — estimated full clinic package

Avoid implying that a small gift alone purchases an item if procurement, exchange rates, or pooled-fund policy does not support that promise.

### 7. The model: assess, install, train, verify

Use four illustrated steps:

1. **Assess** — verify services, load requirements, building condition, solar exposure, and local constraints.
2. **Install** — procure locally where practical, transport safely, and install solar generation, batteries, inverter, regulation, wiring, and clinic equipment.
3. **Train** — prepare local technicians and clinic staff to operate, maintain, and troubleshoot the system.
4. **Verify** — commission the system and report performance at 30 days, 90 days, and one year.

### 8. Meet the team

Show the DRC team as the protagonists. Include concise roles, not long biographies:

- Project lead
- Renewable-energy engineer
- Installation technicians
- Community liaison
- Monitoring and evaluation lead
- Global Solutions Lab / EarthGame support

Add the Global Solutions Lab Tournament win as third-party context, and clearly describe EarthGame as the tax-deductible nonprofit receiving donations. Any reference to association with the UN Department of Global Communications must not imply UN endorsement of this campaign.

### 9. Transparency ledger

This section is essential for donor trust. For every clinic, publish:

- Verified budget and supplier quote date
- Amount raised and amount spent
- Equipment list and system design
- Procurement receipts, with private information redacted
- Installation and commissioning dates
- Before and after photos
- Commissioning checklist
- Current operational status
- Maintenance contact
- 30-day, 90-day, and one-year performance updates

Add an email update option: “Follow every clinic from assessment to first light.”

### 10. Final call to action

**Headline:** Help a rural clinic keep the lights on when care cannot wait.

Buttons:

- Donate once
- Give monthly
- Fund a full clinic
- Bring this campaign to your company or foundation

Include tax-deductibility language, payment security, refund/contact information, and a downloadable one-page brief for institutional donors.

## Donation experience

The donation flow should support:

- Preset amounts and custom amount
- One-time and monthly giving
- Full-clinic sponsorship inquiry
- Optional dedication
- Optional public donor recognition
- Donor-covered processing fees only as a transparent opt-in
- Immediate receipt and thank-you page
- Campaign attribution in payment metadata
- Follow-up email showing which milestone comes next

The thank-you page should not end the story. It should show the clinic pipeline, invite sharing, and offer progress updates.

## Visual system

- **Midnight navy:** `#0B1220` — confidence and nighttime context
- **Solar amber:** `#F6B73C` — light, action, and primary CTAs
- **Warm ivory:** `#F7F1E7` — editorial storytelling sections
- **Clinic blue:** `#1D5D8F` — connection to the real Ndingi building
- **Forest green:** `#2B6B57` — verified/completed states

Design language:

- Documentary photography, not stock photography
- Strong editorial typography and asymmetrical grids
- Restrained cards; avoid a dashboard look
- Fine Kongo Central map lines as a background motif
- Honest progress states rather than a theatrical donation thermometer
- Subtle motion: light appearing, progress line drawing, clinic cards entering as they are verified
- Reduced-motion support

## Photography and safeguarding

- Obtain written permission for every identifiable person and clinic interior shown publicly.
- Keep patient records, diagnostic information, and other health information out of photographs.
- Do not photograph patients receiving care unless there is informed, documented consent and a compelling reason.
- Preserve the original files, dates, captions, photographer, clinic, and permission status in an asset register.
- Avoid images chosen primarily to provoke pity. Show agency, technical competence, care, and community ownership.

## Implementation plan

### Phase 1 — Campaign foundations

- Confirm the campaign goal, legal recipient, donation designation, and public reporting commitment.
- Verify the 15-clinic expansion budget and decide whether the public goal is $230,000 or $230,644.
- Complete the asset and consent register for Ndingi.
- Complete profiles for the first 3–4 target clinics.
- Approve English and French campaign copy.

### Phase 2 — Data and content model

- Add a campaign record with goal, amount raised, clinic count, donation URL, and reporting dates.
- Add clinic records using the profile fields above.
- Model milestone states so progress is evidence-based.
- Keep payment totals server-controlled; never trust a client-side progress value.

### Phase 3 — Front-end build

- Create the public Angular route and campaign components.
- Build the hero, pilot story, impact context, clinic rail/map, budget, implementation model, team, ledger, FAQ, and final CTA.
- Implement responsive image delivery, lazy loading below the fold, and a low-bandwidth fallback.
- Add English/French localization.
- Add accessible focus states, headings, alt text, keyboard navigation, contrast, and reduced motion.

### Phase 4 — Donation and reporting

- Create a campaign-specific payment destination and metadata.
- Support one-time and recurring donations.
- Create thank-you and receipt flows.
- Connect verified totals to the campaign progress component.
- Add downloadable PDF brief and sponsor inquiry form.

### Phase 5 — Trust, analytics, and launch QA

- Events: hero CTA, clinic-card view, budget interaction, donation start, donation complete, share, update signup, sponsor inquiry.
- Test mobile, tablet, desktop, slow connections, dark mode interactions if retained, and all payment outcomes.
- Validate structured data, Open Graph image, page title, description, canonical URL, sitemap, and share previews.
- Review every public health, finance, partner, nonprofit, and tax claim before launch.
- Run a privacy and consent review for all media.

## Launch acceptance criteria

- A donor can understand the problem, proof, plan, cost, team, and next action within one scroll and three minutes.
- Every published clinic fact has an internal source and date.
- The donation path is campaign-attributed and works on mobile.
- The page does not imply that Global Solutions Lab or EarthGame is endorsed by the UN.
- The total raised cannot be edited from the browser.
- All identifiable photography has permission recorded.
- English and French versions are complete.
- Page performance remains usable on a slow mobile connection.
- The first transparency update has an owner and publication date before launch.

Approve this narrative and visual direction, then complete the field-verification sheet for the first 3–4 clinics. The actual page can be implemented as soon as those profiles, the donation destination, and the campaign goal are confirmed.
